# Computational Modeling of Political Polarization in Complex Systems

**Students:** Miriam Crisman
**Supervisor:** Eric Araújo
**Term:** Fall 2026 to Spring 2027
**Status:** In progress

## Abstract

This project develops a computational model, built with agent-based modeling tools, to explore how the social fabric of a community fractures under polarization. The study involves (1) understanding the different types of polarization, (2) how behaviors, opinions, and sentiments spread through one-to-one relationships and across a broader network, (3) how individual traits such as openness and expressiveness affect the way agents perceive one another in a computational model, (4) the construction of a mathematical framework grounded in the literature and in data from different sources, (5) the refinement of a framework in which three aspects drive people into or out of communities, namely behavior, belief, and sense of belonging, and (6) the implementation of the agent-based model in AnyLogic, after a prototype built in NetLogo.

The main goal is to validate the model qualitatively against reports from communities that struggle with polarization and have established practices of dialogue. Data is being collected in Kenya, Brazil, and Oman as the project progresses. The reports from these communities will substantiate the model and point to aspects that need to be reconsidered. As a final product, the project will deliver an agent-based model that allows a broader evaluation of polarization dynamics in communities.
