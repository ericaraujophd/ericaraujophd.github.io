# Data-Oriented Agent-Based Model of Polarization and Fracture in Kenyan Society

**Students:** Adam Byle, Alayna Smith
**Supervisor:** Eric Araújo
**Term:** Fall 2026 to Spring 2027
**Status:** In progress

## Abstract

Political polarization and the fracturing of communities are not confined to the northern hemisphere. Countries such as Kenya, Brazil, Argentina, India, and Oman, among many others, also struggle to navigate an age in which technology spreads information faster and less reliably than before. Describing this phenomenon through a single lens would be reductionist; each culture, country, and context needs to be studied on its own terms. A previous agent-based model provides a broad overview of political polarization in communities without going deep into the inner characteristics of any particular context.

The main goal of this project is to extend that generic model and apply it to Kenya. The students will (1) read about Kenya's background and how polarization has been observed there, (2) collect data from reliable sources on Kenya's political, religious, and socioeconomic landscape to inform the model, (3) produce a plan for tailoring the generic model into a more robust and specific model for Kenya, (4) build a data warehouse to support both decision making and the model, and (5) develop a data-oriented agent-based model in which the rules governing the agents are informed by data analysis and machine learning.

The final product is a system in which experiments can be run and results analyzed for the Kenyan context.
