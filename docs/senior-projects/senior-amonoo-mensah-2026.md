# What Is AI Saying about Junia?

**Students:** Sally Amonoo-Mensah
**Supervisor:** Eric Araújo
**Term:** Fall 2026 to Spring 2027
**Status:** In progress

## Abstract

Junia is regarded by some theologians as the first woman to be called an apostle in the early church. We have little more than one verse about her, Romans 16:7, yet this brief mention has been fertile ground for many theories and has shaped the discussion of women's ministry and importance in the first two centuries of the Christian church.

This project evaluates, directly and indirectly, what AI says when asked about Junia. Directly, we can assess the bias, accuracy, and sense of historical relevance that agentic AI tools such as ChatGPT and Claude show when asked simple, difficult, or controversial questions about her. Indirectly, since the context these models draw on includes the books, articles, and other materials that survived into the digital age and form the corpus of past scholarship, prompting AI also offers a glimpse of what scholars have discussed and settled about Junia.

The main goal of this project is to evaluate five or more AI models by (1) studying what scholars have already written about the life of Junia, to the point of becoming an expert on it, (2) developing a question bank for Junia, deciding which questions can reveal the most about how AI sees her, (3) evaluating the models, collecting the data, and applying the rubric from the Matristics project to rank the answers on historical accuracy, coverage, framing and agency, and epistemic quality, and (4) reviewing the rubric and the questions and adding Junia to the Matristics benchmark.
