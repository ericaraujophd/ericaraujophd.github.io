# Computation Modeling of Political Polarization in Complex Systems

**Students:** Miriam Crisman
**Supervisor:** Eric Araújo
**Term:** Fall 2026 to Spring 2027
**Status:** In progress

## Abstract

This project aims to develop a computational model using agent-based modeling tools to explore the fracture in the social fabric of communities facing polarization. The study involves (1) )the understanding of the different types of polarization, (2) how behaviors, opinions and sentiments are spread through relationships one-to-one and in a broader network, (3) how individual traits such as openess and expressiveness affect the way agents perceive each other in a computational model, (4) the construction of a mathematical framework based on literature and data from different sources, (5) the refinement of a framework that considers three aspects as part of what drives people in or our of communities, i.e. behavior, belief, and sense of belonging, and (6) the implementation of an agent-based model on AnyLogic after a prototype was created on Netlogo.

The main goals are to validate the model qualitatively, based on reports from communities who struggle with polarization and have practices of dialogue. Data has been collected in Kenya, Brazil, and Oman as the project progresses. The report from these communities will substantiate our model and assist us on aspects that need to be reconsidered. As a final product, this project will provide an agent-based model that permits a broader evaluation of this study.
